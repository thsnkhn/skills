## Reading This Reference

- **Observation** means something visible in the account analytics or a sampled public post.
- **Inference** means a drafting decision derived from those observations.
- Public engagement counts change over time and were captured only as visible on the observation date.
- External posts are not normalized for audience size, post age, distribution, paid reach, format, or creator reputation. Their counts are directional evidence, not controlled comparisons.
- A public high-engagement post is not necessarily the creator's all-time top post.
- Post descriptions below are summaries, not reproductions of the original copy.
- The account owner's name, profile URL, byline, screenshots, and browser metadata are intentionally omitted.

### Strongest observed patterns

1. **A real invitation with a reason to act**

   The strongest engagement came from a specific offline event, not a generic engagement question.

2. **A concise opinion with an operational consequence**

   Several high-ranked posts make one clear claim and explain what it means for founders, teams, or products.

3. **A public stake in something being built**

   Product ideas and username reservations ranked strongly in this sample when they exposed actual momentum and gave readers something concrete to respond to.

4. **A milestone supported by proof**

   Awards, releases, working software, and measurable outcomes appeared among the high-ranked posts when they were paired with proof rather than abstract ambition.

5. **Playfulness attached to real work**

   In the high-ranked playful posts, informal phrasing and dry humor were attached to an actual decision, artifact, or result rather than used as a substitute for substance.

6. **Rawness can be an asset**

   A lightly edited builder note ranked strongly when it included a specific product, a surprising build result, and immediate proof.

## External Public Benchmarks

### Jason Fried: disagreement during product work

Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jason-fried_ive-never-argued-with-an-ai-like-ive-argued-activity-7428944308059230208-uqFA)

- **Visible engagement:** 852 reactions, 84 comments, 27 reposts.
- **Content mode:** Product-work observation.
- **Observation:** One coherent paragraph turns a specific experience into a compact product philosophy. It has no hashtag bundle, generic question, or CTA.
- **Inference:** A distinctive observation from real work can carry the post. Preserve the strange or vivid detail instead of forcing it into a standard template.

### Jason Fried: long career reflection

Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jason-fried_ive-consulted-ive-done-client-work-ive-activity-7148461399663329280-HNQZ)

- **Visible engagement:** 1,801 reactions and 133 comments; repost count was not captured.
- **Content mode:** Personal narrative.
- **Observation:** The post follows a long professional arc, uses concrete contrasts, and is substantially longer than a typical update.
- **Inference:** Length should follow the story. Long writing works when each section advances a real experience rather than repeating a lesson.

### David Heinemeier Hansson: cloud-spend chart

Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/david-heinemeier-hansson-374b18221_this-is-our-cloud-spend-over-the-last-12-activity-7142603347013844992-MseI)

- **Visible engagement:** 8,777 reactions, 895 comments, 469 reposts.
- **Content mode:** Milestone with proof and opinion.
- **Observation:** The main copy is compact, the chart makes the financial change visible, and the conclusion follows directly from completed operational work.
- **Inference:** Strong opinions become more credible after the work is done. Put inspectable proof next to the claim and let it carry the confidence.

### Guillermo Rauch: one-sentence software thesis

Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rauchg_all-software-will-be-generative-and-generated-activity-7415109755519787008-feKN)

- **Visible engagement:** 770 reactions, 79 comments, 65 reposts.
- **Content mode:** Ultra-short thesis.
- **Observation:** A single clear prediction carries the entire post without a CTA, supporting list, or hashtag bundle.
- **Inference:** Do not inflate a complete thought. One sentence is a valid finished post when the idea is sharp enough.

### Gergely Orosz: timely industry development

Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gergelyorosz_well-this-is-massive-with-lots-of-implications-activity-7471491467199512576-1HTh)

- **Visible engagement:** 1,982 reactions and 173 comments; repost count was not captured.
- **Content mode:** Timely industry analysis.
- **Observation:** The post identifies a current development, names specific downstream implications, cites a source, and leaves room for uncertainty.
- **Inference:** A timely post should add interpretation. Merely repeating the news is weaker than explaining who it affects and what may change.

### Georgia Gibson: personal next chapter

Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/georgia-gibson-a89b57163_my-next-chaptah-activity-7402383706935500803-x3Nh)

- **Visible engagement:** 4,411 reactions, 1,959 comments, 23 reposts.
- **Content mode:** Personal story and announcement.
- **Observation:** The post combines a genuinely unusual event, chronology, tension, concrete scenes, emotional stakes, and an unmistakable idiolect. A keyword CTA contributes to the high comment count.
- **Inference:** Personal narrative performs when the event is worth telling and the language sounds owned. Treat CTA-driven comments separately from pure story resonance, and do not manufacture vulnerability or imitate another writer's verbal quirks.

## Cross-sample Observations

- High engagement appears across one sentence, one paragraph, a chart-backed milestone, timely analysis, and long narrative formats.
- A mandatory hook-body-CTA structure is not shared by the sampled posts.
- Generic engagement questions, hashtag bundles, and forced line-by-line formatting are not necessary for strong response.
- The repeated ingredients are a real event or belief, specificity, evidence, and a recognizable point of view.
- The strongest posts stop once the point is complete.

## First-comment Evidence

### What was observed

- The account recorded 81 link engagements and 27 visits to links in posts during the analytics window.
- The available analytics do not separate links placed in the post body from links placed in the first comment.
- The cloud-spend benchmark kept the main post self-contained, then used author comments for the underlying [migration article](https://world.hey.com/dhh/we-have-left-the-cloud-251760fb) and a later [FAQ](https://world.hey.com/dhh/the-big-cloud-exit-faq-20274010).
- Those comments extended the post with useful material; they do not establish that comment placement caused the main post's reach.
- No controlled comparison was performed between body links and first-comment links.

### What can be inferred

- A first comment is useful as **supporting infrastructure**, not as a guaranteed distribution tactic.
- Put an article, application, demo, repository, FAQ, source, or extended context in the first comment when the URL would interrupt the main story.
- The main post must remain complete and understandable without opening the comment.
- Do not claim that first-comment links improve reach unless current platform-specific evidence establishes it.
- If the link is the primary action or withholding it creates friction, include it in the main post instead.
- When used, return a complete paste-ready first comment rather than a placeholder or URL dump.

## Drafting Implications

1. Begin with the most alive fact, decision, result, or opinion, not a stock hook.
2. Select the mode from the source material: raw builder note, operator opinion, milestone with proof, invitation or action, or personal narrative.
3. Include concrete nouns, numbers, artifacts, people, decisions, or outcomes.
4. Add the point of view earned by the evidence.
5. Keep natural paragraphs and preserve informal or playful phrasing when it is authentic.
6. Let the substance determine length.
7. Do not require a CTA, question, emoji, hashtag, or first comment.
8. Stop when the point lands.
9. Refresh this evidence only when current performance materially affects the assignment and the user explicitly requests a repository update.
